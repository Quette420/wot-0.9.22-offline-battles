# v1.2.0 - Bot combat and aiming overhaul

## Bot combat and aiming

### Finding a target and a firing point

- Direct-fire Bots start with the nearest living enemy they can currently observe through spotting or radio reports. Lower nominal armor breaks an exact distance tie. The controller checks each candidate with the mounted gun and shell's actual range. It can check other enemies with the same shell when a closer one is blocked or too well armored.
- A penetration firing point needs a clear projectile path, reachable gun angles, and at least a **65% estimated chance to penetrate structural armor**. The controller checks individual points using the armor geometry and impact angle, and leads moving targets using their motion and the shell's flight time. Terrain, wrecks, and vehicles can block individual points. A point can be reconsidered when its lane clears.
- Bots can review and track firing points while following their route. A usable opportunity on the same enemy with the same shell must persist for **two seconds** before combat takes over route movement. The point may change within that enemy. The vehicle then completes its stop, hull turn, and gun alignment. An established engagement can pass directly to another verified target after a kill.
- A verified aim point stays selected while it remains usable. When a lane closes, the controller can check other points on the same vehicle. If a physical obstruction or mechanical limit calls for a new firing position, it checks a route and firing lane for the same target, shell, and point. The controller checks the actual position again on arrival and resumes the original task if recovery runs out of usable positions or stalls. A vehicle parked inside a capture circle can continue aiming under the parking constraints.
- Armor review and launch checks are separate. Armor is reviewed again when either the three-dimensional angle of the line between the vehicles or the enemy hull's horizontal heading changes by **30 degrees** from the last review. The first shot of each automatic trigger still checks the current barrel, trajectory, loading state, and risk to allies.

### Shell selection and HE fallback

- The loaded shell gets the first chance to find a usable penetration point. When its review finds no qualifying penetration option, the controller tries the next stocked penetration round in ascending penetration order. Listed damage and shell slot break ties. An obstructed lane, a gun-angle limit, and a pending check each have their own handling.
- **HE fallback uses a high-explosive shell's contact and blast damage** when the penetration sequence yields no usable option. The controller prefers the lowest-penetration stocked HE for this role. With ordinary HE loaded, it first looks for a usable penetration point on the current target. If the clear points fail the penetration check, it compares the expected vehicle HP loss for legal contacts on that vehicle.
- The fallback comparison can finish over multiple updates. The winning contact is rechecked against the current firing lane before the shot. Barrel and track contacts are evaluated by the same damage rules. A verified contact with zero expected vehicle HP loss can still qualify if it meets the physical launch checks.
- A single-shot gun can return to the lowest-penetration stocked penetration round after **two consecutive HE fallback rounds actually fire**. A penetrating HE shot resets that count. Current evidence that the lowest-penetration round still cannot penetrate defers the return. Magazine guns apply the return at the full reload boundary.

### HESH primary ammunition

The following gun and shell pairs take part in the full penetration review. In these loadouts HESH is the primary round and is tried before its higher-penetration AP or APCR backup. The whitelist matches the installed gun and shell. HESH still uses HE damage rules after impact.

| Vehicle | Gun | HESH penetration |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| FV215b (183) | 183 mm L4 | 230 mm |
| FV4005 | 183 mm AT Gun | 230 mm |
| Centurion 7/1 | 105 mm Gun L7 | 210 mm |
| Charioteer | 105 mm AT Gun L7 | 210 mm |
| FV4004 Conway | 5.5-inch AT Gun | 200 mm |

For example, the FV4005 checks penetration with its stocked HESH Mk.1, then tries its AP backup if that HESH cannot find a penetration point. Low-penetration HE comes into play at fallback. If HESH is the only HE remaining, it can also be used for contact damage after the penetration review.

### Magazines and multi-round triggers

- **Magazine reloads no longer trigger an automatic tactical retreat.** A Bot with a usable position and target can reload in place and keep aiming. Verified obstruction and collision recovery can still move the vehicle when needed.
- The current magazine is kept while it has a usable shot. The next load is prepared separately and normally takes effect after the last trigger of the magazine. If a complete review finds no valid penetration point for the loaded penetration magazine, the gun can qualify for an early full reload to the next penetration round or to HE fallback. A loaded ordinary HE magazine can use its remaining rounds on penetration points or legal blast contacts.
- **One trigger and one magazine are separate.** An ordinary magazine gun can fire one round per trigger. A machine gun or burst gun can fire several rounds at its configured interval. For example, a three-round trigger fires two rounds if only two remain in the magazine.
- The first round of a new automatic trigger must pass the fire checks. After that round commits, the weapon completes the admitted burst at its own cadence. A target change, new obstruction, or release of automatic control affects the next trigger. Each remaining round uses its own actual barrel pose and dispersion, spends one round of ammunition, and receives its own collision and damage result. Physical weapon failure, vehicle destruction, or the end of the battle can end the burst.
- Fixed an end-of-magazine settlement error where the last fired shell could be identified as the type already selected for the next magazine. The fired type and next load are now tracked separately.

### Siege, artillery, and Bot difficulty

- Siege vehicles request siege after combat takes control and use the actual enabled mode, chassis support, and hydraulic pose for laying. A confirmed hydraulic aim can stay on the same target during a temporary recheck. Fixed cases where Strv S1 and UDES 03 lost the point and repeatedly reset the barrel within the same engagement.
- Artillery ranks reachable targets by expected vehicle HP damage from its actual HE trajectory. It checks firing-position occupancy and settles before aiming. Shell cycling uses settled projectile results again. Two settled rounds with neither a direct hit nor damage to an enemy can advance to another stocked HE round. A direct hit or damaging splash clears that streak. Magazine guns change shell type at the full reload boundary.
- Removed artificial reaction delays, aim offsets, lead errors, aiming-circle acceptance modifiers, and skill-rating restrictions on weak-point selection and shell choice. Difficulty is expressed through actual vehicle development, crew, equipment, directives, and consumables.
- Applied the central accuracy change to the offline dispersion distribution. The inner 10% of the aiming circle's radius receives 10% of shots, with the displaced probability moved into the adjacent band. Bot path checks and actual launches use the same dispersion sampler.

## New Testing tab

- Select detailed diagnostics, collision logs, and single-player scenarios from the main launcher. Scenarios include **15 vs. 15 mobile combat**, equipment and crew comparisons, the Bot governor test, a stationary-target earnings test, and the shot-triggered drop test. Start them with the normal launcher button. A scenario loads its own required map and roster.
- Optional player automatic control lives in this tab and shares the new Bot combat rules. Driving, aiming and firing, and consumable use can be enabled independently. Enable takeover at battle start or toggle it in battle with the grave key to the left of `1`. Each LAN player's control runs in their own client.
- The player control panel supports attack, return to base, base defense, hold position, capture, halt, timed driving, selected-shell loading, held aim, and a single trigger. **Fire once** completes one trigger, including the configured rounds of a burst gun.
- With automatic fire enabled at battle start, a direct-fire player vehicle begins with the lowest-penetration stocked penetration round, including eligible HESH. The reviewed FV4005 loadout therefore opens with HESH Mk.1. Driving-only control loads in garage order.

## Fixes and stability

- Fixed a recurring ammunition warning after cancelling a LAN queue request. Fixed opening the location of an exported error report in File Explorer.
- Connected equipment and crew effects to their live consumers, including spall liners, module protection, toolboxes, repair skills, and directives. Repair speed accounts for available crew, injuries, skills, and equipment. Updated medical and fire-extinguishing effects, fire damage decay, and post-battle treatment of injured crew. Fixed inconsistent governor wear during slower updates.
- Fixed Bot shots that appeared in the background simulation without a server projectile result. Accepted launches use their recorded shell and trajectory for publication and retry.
- Fixed repeated selection of a blocked firing point and loss of a usable point during preparation for a later shot. Fixed cases of vehicles parked inside a capture circle failing to turn and fire or alternating their hull aim between two targets.
- Fixed vehicles turning back toward obsolete temporary waypoints after clearing a bridge, wreck, or occupied junction. Spread delayed Bot physics across later frames and share review work among vehicles to reduce concentrated catch-up work during busy fights.
- Fixed a deferred model refresh after an ammo-rack explosion. Adjusted shutdown order for vehicle models, engine audio, and communications, and corrected normal worker exits being reported as a simulation disconnect.
- Corrected the battle-count milestone text to use the client's regular count formatting.

## Loadouts, garage, and battle earnings

- Updated reviewed fully upgraded guns, including the M3G FT, Pz. 38 (t), Stuart I, and Chi-He, and revised default equipment priorities by vehicle class.
- Elite Bot defaults use available premium penetration rounds and premium HE supported by the installed gun. Special gun rules preserve penetration backups for vehicles such as the SU-100Y and ISU-130. The SU-5's 122 mm gun uses its 420-damage HE in default loadouts.
- Rebuilt offline credits and XP. Credit settlement distinguishes self-spotted damage, damage on another vehicle's spotting, and assistance, then applies each vehicle's earnings coefficient. XP uses actual battle events, including damage, kills, spotting, critical hits, capture, defense, and survival. Added qualifying resistance rewards and aligned regional special-vehicle XP bonuses with their mastery thresholds.
- Fixed buy-and-equip camouflage transactions. The garage uses existing copies first and purchases only the shortage. Restored historical crew retraining calculations for same-class and cross-class transfers. The vehicle editor and Bot lineup editor use consistent Chinese and English vehicle class names.

## Default routes and retired systems

- Removed the old built-in route sources, projection code, and fallback entries. All **41 maps** now use the reviewed default route set for normal battles, the tactics editor, and navigation generation. Mittengard uses its reviewed route plan and local passage refinements.
- Retired the old target score, player and attacker preference, focus quotas, artificial hull angling, tactical retreat, peeking, flanking, and return-to-previous-waypoint behaviors.
- Removed the older approach timer for guns that had been ready without firing, the intermediate eight-second penetration-position search, and the separate gun-angle position search. Checked firing-position recovery is part of the new engagement flow.
- Consolidated missions, route recovery, empty-ammunition handling, and combat decisions into one implementation. Removed the obsolete server decision fallback, the duplicate player controller, historical test-package build workflows, and old source-replacement patches.
